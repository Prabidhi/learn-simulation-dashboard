Your own domain on GitLab Pages

How the Learning Lab moved from a long gitlab.io address to learn.prabidhis.com, for free: two DNS records, an ownership check, and a certificate. Then why the first few minutes looked broken even though nothing was wrong.

Key idea: one record sends visitors to GitLab, a second record proves the name is yours, and only then will GitLab answer for it and fetch a certificate. Each step waits on the one before.

1. The pieces

Four parties take part when someone types learn.prabidhis.com. Tap any of them.

Analogy: you rent a new street sign that points to an existing shop. The CNAME is the sign. The shop (GitLab) won't serve anyone who comes because of the sign until you show proof you own the street name, which is the TXT record. Once it believes you, it orders a lock for the door, the certificate, from Let's Encrypt.

    3. What each setting does

    Four small settings. Each card says what breaks if it is missing or later removed.

    CNAME record

    The street sign

    learn  CNAME  prabidhi.gitlab.io

    "learn.prabidhis.com is another name for prabidhi.gitlab.io." Browsers follow it to GitLab's servers. It points at your user's Pages host, not at the project's long unique address.

    Without it: the name leads nowhere and browsers say the site can't be reached.

    TXT record

    The proof of ownership

    _gitlab-pages-verification-code.learn
  TXT  gitlab-pages-verification-code=…

    A random code only someone who controls the DNS could publish. Without this check, anyone could claim your name for their own GitLab project.

    Keep it: GitLab checks again from time to time and turns the domain off if the code is gone.

    DNS only, not proxied

    Cloudflare's grey cloud

    With the orange cloud, Cloudflare answers visitors itself and shows its own certificate. GitLab then can't complete its own certificate check, which commonly makes setup fail.

    Grey cloud: Cloudflare only answers "where is it?" and visitors talk to GitLab directly.

    Let's Encrypt and Force HTTPS

    The lock on the door

    After verification, GitLab asks Let's Encrypt for a free certificate and renews it automatically. The one issued on 2026-11-07 is valid until 5 Feb 2027.

    Force HTTPS then sends anyone typing http:// to the locked https:// version.

    5. After it works

    Small surprises that are normal, not faults.

    Both addresses keep working

    The old …gitlab.io address still serves the same files. Nothing about the project moved; it simply answers to a second name.

    A short delay after deploys

    After the next deploy, learn.prabidhis.com showed the old files for about 45 seconds while the gitlab.io address was already new. GitLab's servers cache each name separately. Seen

    Saved choices start fresh

    Browsers keep saved settings, like the Lab's Day/Twilight/Dark choice, per address. On the new address it starts unset once. Expected

    The domain must stay paid

    The certificate renews itself, but the domain name doesn't. If prabidhis.com lapses, the new address stops and the gitlab.io one keeps working.
