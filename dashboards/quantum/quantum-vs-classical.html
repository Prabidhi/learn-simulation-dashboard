Quantum vs classical chips: the real difference

Four hands-on scenes. Drag the sliders, press the buttons, and watch what changes.

Bedrock idea: a classical bit is a crowd shouting; a qubit is a single whisper. The crowd is robust, so you can listen any time. The whisper is destroyed by anything that overhears it. Superconductors, freezers and vacuum chambers are just different ways to soundproof the whisper.

1 · Crowd vs whisper: what a bit and a qubit actually are

Turn up the noise on both sides. Read, copy and measure. One side shrugs it off; the other changes the moment you look.

Classical bit (CPU / GPU)

Thousands of electrons stored together. The voltage they create is the bit.
Reading a crowd doesn't change it. Ask again and you get the same answer.

Qubit (QPU)

One quantum object holding a blend of 0 and 1. The arrow shows the blend.
P(0)
P(1)
Takeaway: a classical bit survives noise, reading and copying because it's a majority vote of many electrons. A qubit's blend is real information, but looking at it, copying it, or letting the environment touch it destroys it. That one fact drives everything else on this page.

2 · Does electricity flow through it?

A CPU pushes current through its transistors, in one side and out the other. A superconducting qubit is a closed loop where current sloshes back and forth, like water in a U-tube.

Transistor circuit (CPU / GPU)

Close the switch: electrons stream around the circuit. Friction (resistance) turns into heat.

Superconducting qubit

No wire in, no wire out. A microwave pulse sets paired electrons sloshing between capacitor and junction.
Try switching the qubit to normal metal: resistance acts like friction on a guitar string, so the ringing dies almost immediately and the energy leaks out as heat. That's why chip-based qubits use superconductors. Zero resistance lets the "note" ring long enough to compute with. (Animations are slowed down by about ten billion times.)

3 · Why so cold? Watch the "heat sea" rise

Heat is random jiggling energy. Picture it as a sea level. If the sea rises above the gap between 0 and 1, heat alone starts flipping states. Drag the temperature down.

300 Kroom temperature

Classical bit

Switching energy ≈ 1 electron-volt, the same as heat at about 11,600 K. The gap is enormous.

Superconducting qubit (5 GHz)

Energy gap between |0⟩ and |1⟩ ≈ the same as heat at only 0.24 K. The gap is tiny.
The gaps are drawn the same height so you can compare the sea level. At room temperature, the sea is a puddle for the CPU but floods the qubit. Even outer space (2.7 K) is too hot. Only around 0.01 K, in a dilution refrigerator, does the qubit sit calmly in |0⟩, ready to be used.
